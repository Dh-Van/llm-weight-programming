Round 2 plan. What Round 1 showed:

- **Colour detectors were too strict.** Their per-class means were around 0.01, though the patterns were right: sky blue and deep blue pick out parachute and church, green picks out golf ball and springer, white picks out cassette player and chain saw. Splitting into quadrants mostly produced copies of the same signal (r ≈ 0.8–0.98 between quadrants).
- **Edge filters mostly measured overall image activity, not specific shapes.** Parachute is clearly the smoothest class (about 0.2 against 0.4 for the others), and gas pump, French horn and springer have the most edges. Cassette player has the most horizontal edges.
- **The "fine detail" filter was a mistake.** Its weights don't sum to zero, so it just measured brightness.

So this round uses whole-image features, softer thresholds, and pairs of positive and negative versions of each edge filter:

- **R2F1, colour fractions (20 neurons):** 20 colour detectors averaged over the whole image, with a weak and a strong threshold for each main colour, plus skin, brass, brown and mid-brightness detectors.
- **R2F2, edge statistics (20 neurons):** works at 32×32. It measures how much of the image has weak edges and how much has strong edges, separately for each direction and for the brighter-side and darker-side version. It also includes bright and dark spots, a corrected fine-texture filter that sums to zero, and sky-above-ground versus ground-above-sky colour transitions.
- **R2F3, colour presence (16 neurons):** the strongest 4×4-pixel patch of each saturated colour anywhere in the image. This asks whether a small object of that colour is present at all (a red pump, an orange saw, a white ball, a brass horn, a yellow canopy), even when it takes up little of the frame.
